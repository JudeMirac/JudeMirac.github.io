Why percentile ranking?

The selected Random Forest ranked transactions effectively, but its predicted probabilities were tightly clustered.

Using relative percentile rank instead of fixed probability cutoffs keeps the review queue controlled while still prioritizing the highest-risk transactions.

Analytics workflow

From describing fraud to deciding what to do

01 / Descriptive

Find the patterns

Measure the overall fraud rate and compare fraud behavior across merchant, category, payment method, device type, transaction velocity, and prior chargeback history.

02 / Diagnostic

Explain the signals

Explore feature relationships and engineer interaction features so combined behaviors can be represented rather than treating every signal independently.

03 / Predictive

Rank the risk

Compare Logistic Regression and Random Forest, use class weighting to address imbalance, and evaluate model ranking performance with ROC-AUC and threshold analysis.

Prescriptive layer

Model output becomes an operating policy

Balance fraud capture with review capacity

The policy reserves manual review for 15% of transactions and blocking for the riskiest 5%, allowing the remaining 80% to move through automatically.

This makes the decision rule usable by an operations team because queue size is explicit rather than left to an arbitrary probability threshold.

Validate the ranking

Fraud rates rise from low to high risk tiers, supporting the usefulness of the ranking-based policy. In the project evaluation, review and block tiers captured 61% of fraudulent transactions.

The result demonstrates a decision framework; it is not a claim about production fraud performance.
