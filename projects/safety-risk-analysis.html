Business question

Move from counts to risk

Why raw incident counts are not enough

A process path can report more incidents simply because more hours are worked there. The project pairs incident counts with exposure data so comparisons reflect risk relative to operational activity, not volume alone.

The analysis also separates severity, root causes, and contributing factors so a high-frequency area can be reviewed differently from a lower-frequency but more severe area.

What the pipeline produces

Raw CSV files are loaded into SQLite, standardized in staging views, integrated across site and time dimensions, summarized into contextual distributions, and transformed into reusable risk features for analysis.

Python notebooks sit on top of those layers for exploration, data-health checks, process-path analysis, and interpretation.

What the analysis shows

Three ways to prioritize review

01 / Concentration

Start where risk clusters

The top 10 process paths account for 75.5% of incidents in the project analysis. That concentration creates a practical starting point for deeper review rather than spreading attention evenly across every path.

02 / Root cause

Focus on recurring drivers

The top five root causes account for 76.9% of incidents. Grouping the problem by recurring cause helps connect the analysis to targeted controls, training, engineering changes, or process redesign.

03 / Exposure

Compare risk, not just volume

Exposure-normalized incident rates help distinguish high activity from high risk. This makes site and process-path comparisons more defensible than ranking areas on counts alone.

Data quality & reproducibility

Standardize before comparing

The staging layer normalizes text values, enforces data types, creates consistent field names, and handles duplicates and missing data before those records reach the analysis layer.

Make the workflow repeatable

A main Python runner executes the transformation layers, while separate notebooks handle exploratory analysis, context analysis, process-path analysis, and data-health checks. The structure keeps business logic outside one-off spreadsheet work.

Public-data note: the repository intentionally uses synthetic data because operational safety records cannot be shared publicly. The portfolio value is in the pipeline design, metric logic, analysis method, and reproducibility.
